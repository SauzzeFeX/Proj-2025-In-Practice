          How this works

          Where the list comes from

          We read all 30 chapters of the 2023 “Mandate for Leadership” and wrote down each concrete recommendation: something a president, an agency, or Congress could actually do. Each one has the printed page number and a short exact quote. A script checks that every quote really appears on that page.

          The text used is an archived copy of the full PDF. Heritage’s original download link now goes to a landing page.

          How “closeness” is rated

          Direct match
          Same idea and the same way of doing it.
          Partial match
          Same goal, but a different method or size.
          Broad overlap
          Only the general direction is shared.
          No match found
          No action matching the recommendation was found.

          What “status” means

          Two chapters argue with themselves

          The Trade chapter has one author arguing for tariffs (Peter Navarro) and another arguing for free trade (Kent Lassman). The Export-Import Bank chapter has one author for closing it and one for keeping it. Items from those chapters are labeled with whose view they are.

          How the percentages work

          “Carried out in full or in part” is the share of researched recommendations marked done, done but challenged in court, partly done, or partly blocked. Recommendations not researched yet are left out of the percentage entirely. So are recommendations marked “No public record yet”: nothing published shows action and our searches found none, but much of this kind of work (internal staffing, management, classified matters) happens without a public document. A free script checks the Federal Register every day and flags any new match. Every percentage on the site shows how many items it is based on, because a percentage of a few items can swing a lot.

          Research so far started with the “major” recommendations, so the overall percentage currently leans toward big items. It will shift as smaller items are checked.

          Why other sites give different “percent done” numbers

          Other trackers say roughly half of Project 2025 has been carried out. Their numbers depend on how they split the plan into pieces and what they count as “done,” so they are not directly comparable to ours.

          Source types

          government official reports and notices. court filings and rulings. news reporting. law firm legal summaries. academic universities and research groups. advocacy groups with a point of view; read these with that in mind.

          Limits

          • Splitting the plan into items is a judgment call. Another reader might count more or fewer.
          • “Not researched yet” means not checked. It does not mean nothing happened.
          • Courts move fast. Every finding shows the date it was checked.
          • Facts versus view. The written findings describe what was proposed, done and ruled, in plain factual language. Our view (that this agenda is harmful) shows up only in the color coding, and we say so openly.

          How to check for updates yourself

          Every recommendation has a “Follow this topic” box with ready-made searches. Here is what each one covers:

          • Federal Register: every new federal rule, proposed rule and executive order. If an agency changes a policy, it shows up here first. Sort by newest.
          • Congress.gov: every bill, with its status (introduced, passed the House, signed into law).
          • CourtListener: a free database of federal court filings and rulings, useful for checking whether a policy has been sued over or blocked.
          • News: for context and for things that happen outside official channels. Prefer outlets that link to the documents they describe.

          Each finding also shows the date it was last checked. Anything older than a few months, especially court cases, may have changed.

          Words you may run into

          Executive order
          A written instruction from the president to federal agencies. It is not a law passed by Congress.
          Injunction
          A court order that pauses a policy while the case continues. “Preliminary” means it is early, not final.
          Overrule
          When a court decides an older court decision was wrong and no longer applies.
          Independent agency
          A federal body, like the FTC, that Congress set up to be somewhat separate from the president.
          Rule (regulation)
          A binding requirement an agency writes after public notice and comment.
          Appropriations
          The yearly laws in which Congress decides how much money agencies get.
          Reconciliation
          A budget process that lets some tax and spending bills pass the Senate with a simple majority.
          Appeal
          Asking a higher court to review a ruling.
